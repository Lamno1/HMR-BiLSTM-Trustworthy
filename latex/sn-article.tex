\abstract{
We present ResMemECG, a recurrent architecture for AAMI heartbeat
classification that augments a bidirectional recurrent trunk with Residual
Memory Control (RMC), a hybrid standard/residual memory path, and temporal
attention pooling. On the
conventional patient-disjoint MIT-BIH DS1/DS2 evaluation, ResMemECG reaches
accuracy $0.931$, macro F1 $0.628$, and macro AUC $0.953$, exceeding the
classical, convolutional, recurrent, and attention-based baselines evaluated in
the same study, although that comparison does not isolate architectural effects
because the training recipes are not fully matched. We then ask a stricter question: whether its rare-class behaviour
persists across unseen subjects. In a controlled MIT-BIH-only five-fold
subject-grouped cross-validation, the same flat model family yields fold-first
mean F1 scores of $0.011$ for fusion (F) and $0.232$ for supraventricular (S)
beats, with F1-S ranging from $0.039$ to $0.696$ across folds. These values are
reported as cross-protocol sensitivity, not as a causal effect of the split. On
a separate, weaker mixed-source record-grouped protocol, an exploratory
ResMemECG-MP extension (R2) that adds available V1 morphology and beat-relative
rhythm features through residual class-specific heads produces a modest and
seed-consistent improvement in S/PAC ranking, but does not solve the rare-class
problem, and a pre-specified risk-controlled selective policy on the same
protocol receives a NO-GO result. F is retained as a referral class in this
dataset, single-beat setting, available leads, and tested inter-patient
pipelines. The
study therefore contributes both the ResMemECG model and a subject-grouped
evaluation of where its apparent gains do and do not generalise.
}
\keywords{ResMemECG, ECG arrhythmia classification, residual memory control,

selective prediction}

\section{Introduction}
Automatic ECG classification must combine local waveform morphology with
temporal memory while learning from a severely imbalanced class distribution.
We developed ResMemECG for this setting. Its Residual Memory Control (RMC) cell
learns an interaction-aware correction to the conventional LSTM update, while a
hybrid path retains the standard update as a stable fallback and attention
pooling selects the informative part of each beat. The original objective was
to improve inter-patient five-class AAMI classification without discarding the
sequential inductive bias of a bidirectional recurrent network.

The conventional DS1/DS2 experiment establishes the model-level result, but it
also exposes a second problem. The two rare classes---supraventricular (S) and
ventricular--normal fusion (F)---are supplied predominantly by a few patients.
Consequently, a patient-disjoint split can still yield a patient-composition-
fragile rare-class score. We therefore evaluate ResMemECG twice: first under
the established DS1/DS2 protocol, then under controlled MIT-BIH-only five-fold
subject-grouped cross-validation (SG-CV), which reveals how the result changes
across unseen subject groups. A separate mixed-source record-grouped analysis
is retained for the R2 and selective-prediction diagnostics. These evaluations
define the scope of ResMemECG; they are not a replacement research project.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ResMemECG, comprising an interaction-aware RMC cell, a learnable hybrid
  memory path, and attention pooling for single-beat ECG classification;
  \item a conventional patient-disjoint comparison with classical and deep
  baselines, followed by controlled MIT-BIH-only subject-grouped cross-validation
  of the same flat model family;
  \item a diagnostic extension, ResMemECG-MP (R2), that adds available V1 and
  rhythm-related inputs through residual class-specific heads; it is evaluated
  only on a legacy mixed-source record-grouped protocol and is reported as an
  exploratory sub-study, not as a validated method; and
  \item a scoped rare-class analysis showing that improved S/PAC ranking does
  not by itself establish reliable automatic decisions for unseen subjects, and
  that fusion beats are best routed to expert review in every tested setting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Recurrent memory models for ECG classification}
Convolutional networks capture local waveform structure, while LSTM and BiLSTM
models represent temporal dependencies across the beat. Attention mechanisms
can subsequently weight diagnostically informative time steps
\cite{RN8,RN16}. Prior work has explored the broader LSTM design space
\cite{RN10,RN11}, but standard ECG pipelines generally retain the conventional
cell update. ResMemECG instead places an interaction-aware residual correction
inside the recurrent memory update and blends it with the standard path. This
memory mechanism, rather than the later selective policy, is the architectural
subject of the paper.

\subsection{Inter-patient ECG evaluation and the DS1/DS2 protocol}
The MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database~\cite{RN20} remains a common benchmark for
heartbeat classification. De Chazal et al.~\cite{RN21} established a widely
used patient-disjoint DS1/DS2 protocol. Mousavi et al.~\cite{ref29} and Zhou
et al.~\cite{ref30} also reported sequence-to-sequence and hybrid classifiers
under this split.
These studies are important comparators, but a shared split does not by itself
make a rare-class estimate representative when a small number of patients
provide most test examples. Our work focuses on that remaining evaluation gap.

\subsection{Transformer and edge-oriented ECG classifiers}
Recent CNN--Transformer and lightweight wearable-oriented models continue to
extend ECG classification~\cite{ref31,ref32,li2025modwpt,xu2026cardiolike}.
CardioLike-Net is particularly relevant as a recent BSPC inter-patient and
edge-oriented comparator, although its task construction and hardware objective
differ from ours. Their evaluation protocols range
from patient-disjoint to intra-patient or stratified settings, so their headline
scores are not directly interchangeable. We therefore compare evaluation
designs explicitly rather than treating heterogeneous published scores as a
single leaderboard.

\subsection{Rare classes, calibration, and selective prediction}
Class imbalance~\cite{RN5}, patient-specific morphology, and uncertainty
estimation~\cite{RN19} complicate rare-class decisions. Calibration and
selective prediction can separate ranking ability from operational acceptance,
but their validity depends on the calibration unit and on distributional
stability. A recent BSPC benchmark compared 11 uncertainty methods for Holter
AF detection and likewise evaluated reject inference rather than confidence in
isolation~\cite{rahman2026uq}. Recent explainable ECG studies combine
spatio-temporal models with post-hoc attribution, while a 2026 BSPC systematic
review documents the continuing lack of standardised, clinician-validated ECG
explanation benchmarks~\cite{abousaber2025explainable,taleban2026xai}. These
works motivate comparison with our attention and gate inspection, but do not
make an attention map a clinically validated explanation. We evaluate realised
fold-, subject-, and record-level behaviour rather than assuming that a
beat-level nominal target transfers to unseen subjects.

\subsection{Problem formulation and training objective}
Given an ECG beat $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$, ResMemECG learns a mapping to the five
AAMI classes N, S, V, F, and Q. Each single-lead segment has $T=187$ samples.
Training uses class-weighted focal loss,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\sum_k w_k
\mathbf{1}[y_i=k](1-p_{i,k})^{\gamma}\log p_{i,k},\qquad \gamma=1.5,
\end{equation}
with weights computed from the training partition only. This objective is one
component of the fixed ResMemECG recipe; the later subject-grouped comparison
holds the whole recipe constant and does not isolate the contribution of this
term.

\subsection{CNN, recurrent trunk, and attention pooling}
Two Conv1D--batch-normalisation--ReLU--max-pooling blocks extract local
morphology and reduce the temporal length from 187 to 46 while expanding the
representation to 64 channels. A two-layer bidirectional recurrent trunk then
produces a 192-dimensional sequence representation. Attention pooling learns
weights over recurrent time steps before a layer-normalised, dropout-regularised
classifier produces the five logits. The complete model contains approximately
505,000 trainable parameters.

\subsection{Residual Memory Control}
A standard LSTM forms its cell update from a retained-memory term and a proposed
update. ResMemECG adds an interaction-aware residual gate
\begin{equation}
r_t=\sigma\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(W_c c_{t-1}+W_h h_{t-1}+m_t)\right),
\qquad m_t=(W_c c_{t-1})\odot(W_h h_{t-1}).
\end{equation}
The gate modulates a keep stream derived from the previous cell state and an add
stream derived from the candidate update. After layer normalisation, learned
softmax weights combine the two streams. This is a learned residual correction,
not an exact algebraic decomposition of the standard LSTM update.

\subsection{Hybrid memory path}
To retain the stable behaviour of a conventional LSTM, the RMC update runs in
parallel with the standard cell update:
\begin{equation}
\beta_t=\sigma(W_\beta h_{t-1}),\qquad
c_t=\beta_t\odot c_t^{\mathrm{rmc}}+(1-\beta_t)\odot c_t^{\mathrm{lstm}}.
\end{equation}
The blend bias is initialised to $-5$, so the network begins close to a standard
LSTM and can increase its use of the RMC path during optimisation. A temporal
smoothness term discourages abrupt changes in $r_t$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}+
\frac{\lambda}{T-1}\sum_{t=2}^{T}\lVert r_t-r_{t-1}\rVert_2^2,
\qquad \lambda=0.003.
\end{equation}
The single-seed ablation evidence for the interaction and smoothness terms is
direction-mixed; they are therefore described as design components rather than
independently proven sources of improvement.

\subsection{ResMemECG-MP diagnostic extension (R2)}
ResMemECG-MP is a diagnostic add-on, not part of the primary ResMemECG forward
path of Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}; ``R2'' denotes the second rung of the
screening ladder that produced it. It is evaluated only on the legacy
mixed-source record-grouped protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:rgcv}). It preserves the ResMemECG morphology trunk and shared
five-class head. A lightweight encoder of the available V1 signal produces an
additive correction to the F logit, while an MLP over beat-relative RR features
produces an additive correction to the S logit. Both residual heads are
zero-initialised. Because that study does not contain a matched V1-only
ablation, the observed S/PAC change is attributed to the extension as a whole,
not specifically to its rhythm branch.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Datasets and AAMI label mapping}
MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database records form the primary evaluation source. Beats
are centred on the annotated R peak and mapped to the five AAMI groups N, S, V,
F, and Q. The principal morphology input is the MLII lead when it is available;
the R2 configuration additionally uses the available V1-channel signal. INCART
records enter only the legacy mixed-source protocol of Section~\ref{sec:rgcv},
where they provide supporting record and morphology diversity, with lead II as
the principal morphology channel and the available V1 lead for the auxiliary
path. Because INCART and MIT-BIH differ in acquisition and population, all
results are stratified by source. MIT-BIH alone determines the primary
conclusions and the selective-prediction GO/NO-GO decision; INCART is supporting
evidence only and is absent from the controlled subject-grouped study.

The INCART preprocessing below therefore applies only to the legacy protocol.
Preprocessing preserves record identity through every stage. INCART signals
undergo source-specific baseline filtering, R-peak recentering, polarity
correction, and a morphology-quality filter before entering the combined pool.
No globally normalised waveform is stored. Normalisation is fitted afresh
inside each outer fold as described below, preventing information from an outer
test record from entering its training transformation.
\subsection{Conventional DS1/DS2 reference evaluation}
\label{sec:ds2ref}
For literature continuity, we retain the patient-disjoint DS1/DS2 partition
introduced by de Chazal et al.~\cite{RN21}. DS1 supplies model development data
and DS2 is held out for evaluation. Although this protocol prevents the same
record from appearing on both sides of the split, its rare-class test support is
highly concentrated: 93\% of its F beats come from patient 213 and 75\% of its S
beats come from patient 232. Consequently, its F and S scores are sensitive to
the behaviour of those particular records. We use DS1/DS2 as a conventional
reference rather than treating its single rare-class estimate as a patient
distribution.

\subsection{Controlled MIT-BIH subject-grouped cross-validation}
\label{sec:sgcv}
The primary generalisation check is MIT-BIH-only five-fold subject-grouped
cross-validation (SG-CV). After excluding the paced records used neither by the
conventional benchmark nor by this analysis, 44 records form 43 subject groups;
records 201 and 202, which belong to one subject, are bound to the same group.
Each outer fold holds out eight or nine complete subject groups, and the
model-train, inner-validation, and outer-test groups are disjoint. This is not
traditional leave-one-patient-out evaluation because an outer fold contains
multiple subjects.

Subject groups are ordered by their F and S beat counts and allocated by a
snake-draft procedure. This preserves the subject as the indivisible unit while
distributing the largest rare-class clusters across folds. Exact balance is not
possible without splitting subjects: outer-fold F support is
376/367/19/17/23 beats and S support is 210/547/521/1,463/39. We therefore
report every fold and first average the two training seeds within each fold;
beats are never treated as independent replications.

The controlled run uses the flat two-layer ResMemECG classifier, MIT-BIH only,
seeds 42 and 123, a 45-epoch maximum with patience 15, the same cosine schedule,
focal objective and FGSM training recipe, and exactly 37,208 model-training N
beats in every fold. Waveform normalisation is fitted on model-train data only.
The best epoch is selected solely by inner-validation macro-F1 over N/S/V/F;
the outer test fold is not used to select an epoch, threshold, temperature, or
model parameter.

\subsection{Mixed-source record-grouped diagnostic protocol}
\label{sec:rgcv}
The R2, forensic, and selective-prediction analyses predated SG-CV and use a
separate five-fold record-grouped protocol (RG-CV). Its pool contains 44
MIT-BIH and 36 INCART records, uses the ECG record as the splitting unit, a
25-epoch schedule, and a 35,000-beat model-training N cap. Records are ordered
by F and S count and assigned by snake draft. MIT-BIH and INCART test metrics
are always source-stratified, and MIT-BIH alone determines the selective
GO/NO-GO decision.

RG-CV is record-disjoint but is not guaranteed subject-disjoint. In particular,
MIT-BIH records 201 and 202 occur in different RG-CV folds, and the pipeline
does not have an INCART subject-identity map. We consequently retain RG-CV only
as the matched flat--R2 and selective diagnostic experiment and do not present
it as leave-one-patient-out or as the primary controlled generalisation result.

\subsection{Inner validation and conformal calibration in RG-CV}

Each outer iteration contains three mutually record-disjoint development
partitions. The \emph{model-train} records fit the network parameters. The
\emph{inner-validation} records select the best epoch using macro-F1 over
\{S,V,F\}; the same frozen inner-validation predictions subsequently serve as
the tuning data for per-class temperature fitting. A separate
\emph{conformal-calibration} partition is carved only from S-bearing, F-free
records. This restriction protects the already small pool of F-bearing records
from being removed from model training and makes explicit that the conformal
procedure is evaluated for S, whereas F analysis is referral-diagnostic only.
None of these partitions contains an outer-test record, and no threshold,
temperature, epoch, or model parameter is fitted on the outer test fold.

Waveform mean and standard deviation are estimated separately for each database
source using model-train records only, then applied unchanged to model-train,
inner-validation, conformal-calibration, and outer-test beats. Training follows
the same recurrent recipe for the flat and R2 configurations. Best-epoch
predictions selected by inner-validation \{S,V,F\} macro-F1 are the primary
outputs. Tail-epoch stochastic weight averaging was also evaluated, with batch
normalisation statistics recomputed on model-train data, but it did not improve
R2 and is not used for the primary comparison.

Before committing GPU resources to all RG-CV outer folds, candidate configurations
were screened on one fixed sentinel split containing difficult, high-support F
and S records from both databases. A candidate advanced to full two-seed RG-CV
evaluation only if it improved a sentinel rare class without reducing N or V
relative to the preceding retained configuration. The sentinel was a screening
device only; all paper conclusions use the complete outer-fold evaluation.
\subsection{Statistical units and reporting}
Outer-fold values, not pooled beats, are the primary generalisation summaries.
In SG-CV, the subject group is the splitting unit. In legacy RG-CV analyses,
the record is the splitting and resampling unit because subject identity is not
available consistently. The two training seeds for a fold are paired repetitions
on the same held-out groups and are not independent observations. We report both
seed values, their within-fold mean and spread, and all five fold means. R2--flat
comparisons are paired by RG-CV fold and seed. Record-cluster uncertainty keeps
all beats from a selected record together; independent-beat bootstrap is not
used as primary uncertainty evidence. MIT-BIH and INCART remain separate. With
five folds and two non-independent seeds per protocol, we do not compute
population-level significance tests; all fold-level comparisons in this paper
are descriptive, and directional statements (for example ``every fold'' or
``four of five folds'') refer to the observed folds only.

\section{Main Results}
\subsection{ResMemECG under the conventional DS1/DS2 protocol}
On the 49,669-beat DS2 test set, ResMemECG achieved accuracy $0.931$, macro
F1 $0.628$, weighted F1 $0.933$, and macro AUC $0.953$
(Table~\ref{tab:performance}). It exceeded every baseline trained in the study
on these aggregate metrics; the strongest comparator macro F1 was $0.452$ from the
TCN. This is the primary model result under the conventional protocol. It does
not establish uniform rare-class generalisation, which is assessed separately
by the controlled subject-grouped cross-validation in the following subsections.

\subsection{What the forensic evidence does not establish}

F-specific inter-annotator agreement statistic. A transparent rule proxy reached
F1 $0.051$ and Cohen's $\kappa=0.022$, but this is a measurement of that rule

\section{Selective Prediction and Referral}
\label{sec:selective}
\subsection{Pre-specified decision policy and operating criterion}
For each fold and seed, a vector of class-specific temperatures is fitted on
inner-validation logits and then frozen. On the separate conformal-calibration
records, the S nonconformity score is one minus the calibrated probability of S
for true-S beats. Its split-conformal quantile defines an S-calibrated threshold
at each pre-specified miscoverage level. Applying that threshold to all class
probabilities produces a prediction set. A beat is automatically decided only
when the set is a singleton and its singleton class is not F; singleton-F and
all non-singleton or empty sets are referred. F-related quantities are therefore
referral diagnostics, never the result of searching for an ``optimal F
threshold.'' Realised S coverage on every outer fold is reported alongside its
nominal target because held-out-record shift weakens the exchangeability assumption.

The useful-operating-point criterion was fixed before the final continuous
risk--coverage curves were inspected. For a MIT-BIH fold, we consider the point
at which the overall selective risk among all accepted automatic decisions is
at most 0.02. The fold has a useful S operating point only if, at that point,
the class-conditional auto-S coverage
$P(\mathrm{accept}\land\widehat{Y}=S\mid Y=S)$ is at least 0.15 and the auto-S
precision $P(Y=S\mid\mathrm{accept}\land\widehat{Y}=S)$ is at least 0.90.
The S selective contribution receives a GO verdict only if at least two folds
from the pre-specified ranking-reliable set \{1,2,3\} meet all three conditions.
Fold 0 is excluded because its S ranking AUC is 0.73
(Section~\ref{sec:s-forensic}), and fold 4 is excluded
because 55\% of its S support comes from one patient. These exclusions affect
the GO count but do not remove either fold from the reported results.

The selective layer receives a NO-GO verdict as an S automation contribution.
For F, it was never evaluated as a threshold-optimisation mechanism: every
singleton-F decision is referred by construction, and the reported F quantities
describe referral workload and leakage only. Combined with the low-rank F errors
and patient-relative geometry, this supports an operational recommendation to
refer F-labelled candidates in this dataset, single-beat setting, available
leads, and tested inter-patient pipelines. It is not a claim that all possible
fusion-beat classifiers or decision policies must fail.
\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What ResMemECG achieves, and where it remains limited}
Under the conventional DS1/DS2 protocol, ResMemECG is the strongest model in
our evaluated set on accuracy, macro F1, weighted F1, and macro AUC. The RMC and
hybrid memory design therefore remains the central model contribution of this
work. The subject-grouped results qualify, rather than erase, that contribution:
aggregate performance on one fixed split does not imply that every rare class
generalises uniformly across unseen subject groups.

The R2 extension supplies the clearest positive rare-class result. It improves
S/PAC ranking against the matched flat ResMemECG configuration in all five
MIT-BIH folds, with a positive mean F1-S change and comparable seed spread. At
the same time, calibration did not convert those ranked scores into a useful
selective operating point under the registered policy. For F, the probability-
decile and geometry analyses show that the remaining errors are not merely
high-ranked examples narrowly losing the argmax. These results define the
observed boundary of the present model and inputs; they are not presented as a
catalogue of failed architectures or as a universal limit on future methods.

\subsection{Explanations that remain compatible with the evidence}
The present analyses cannot distinguish among F label ambiguity, missing rhythm
or lead context, and insufficient diversity in the small set of F-bearing
patients. Low-rank F errors are compatible with missing discriminative signal,
but do not identify why that signal is absent. Patient-relative N/V geometry is
compatible with an annotation boundary that depends on local rhythm context,
but does not establish that interpretation. Likewise, the lack of independent
annotator labels prevents separation of label disagreement from limitations of
the recorded inputs or the tested models. These possibilities should therefore
remain alternatives rather than being collapsed into a claim that F has an
intrinsic performance ceiling.

\subsection{Questions not tested here}
We did not evaluate probability-threshold acceptance as an alternative to the
conformal singleton rule, learned sequence or rhythm-context models, systematic
scaling of the number of records, or the R2 configuration under the conventional
DS1/DS2 protocol. The last comparison would require the deferred D1 experiment.
These are genuine open questions, not explanations supported by the present
results. In particular, the P2 NO-GO applies to the pre-specified policy on
these data; it is not a proof that every selective policy must fail.

\subsection{Ranking improvement versus useful automatic decisions}
The practical result is the separation between ranking and decision utility.
R2 improved S-AUC over flat in every MIT-BIH fold, including folds in which the
flat model was near chance. Yet the same scores did not produce a useful
risk-controlled operating point on any of the three pre-specified reliable
folds. Better ranking is therefore insufficient evidence that a classifier can
be trusted to make automatic decisions for held-out records. Evaluation must
carry the ranked scores through calibration, acceptance, record-level
heterogeneity, and the intended error constraint before an operational claim is
made.

For F, referral is consequently a design recommendation for this dataset,
single-beat setting, available leads, and tested inter-patient pipelines. It
has not been validated as a clinical policy. Its purpose is to avoid
presenting unreliable automatic F decisions as successful automation
while preserving a clear route to expert review.

\section{Limitations}
The controlled comparison reduces three major confounds in the earlier analysis:
SG-CV is MIT-BIH-only, uses the 45-epoch recipe, and fixes model-training N at
37,208. It nevertheless does not identify a causal effect of the split. The
DS1/DS2 value is one fixed reference result, whereas SG-CV contains five refitted
models per seed with different training and test subjects. The observed
$0.241$-to-$0.0109$ F1-F gap is therefore protocol-sensitivity evidence, not a
decomposition of how much performance is caused by patient composition alone.

SG-CV contains two training seeds and five outer folds, with eight or nine test
subject groups per fold. Rare-class support remains intrinsically uneven, most
visibly for F (376/367/19/17/23 beats). Seeds repeat optimisation on the same
held-out subjects and are not independent statistical units. Five folds also do
not provide a precise population-level variance estimate; the individual fold
values remain essential to interpretation. The mixed-source RG-CV confidence
intervals are record-cluster intervals and are wide by construction.

The R2 extension and the selective-prediction analysis were only run under the
legacy mixed-source record-grouped protocol, which is record-disjoint but not
guaranteed subject-disjoint. They were not repeated under controlled SG-CV. Their
results (the S/PAC ranking gain and the selective NO-GO) should therefore be read
as exploratory findings on a weaker protocol, and a confirmatory SG-CV run of
both is left for future work.

MIT-BIH distributes consensus annotations rather than independent annotator
labels. No F-specific inter-annotator agreement ceiling was available, so the
study cannot quantify annotation ambiguity or use the rule proxy as such a
ceiling. Alternative selective policies, including direct probability-threshold
acceptance, and learned sequence-context models were not evaluated. The
selective NO-GO is restricted to the pre-specified temperature-plus-Mondrian
singleton policy on these data.

INCART appears only in the separate mixed-source RG-CV development pool and
supplies supporting evidence about PAC rankability and record diversity. It is
absent from controlled SG-CV and did not enter the selective GO decision.
Finally, conformal calibration
relies on exchangeability that is stressed by evaluation on held-out record
groups and by the small calibration record count. We consequently report
realised and nominal coverage separately and do not present the nominal target
as a guarantee under record shift.

\section{Conclusion}
ResMemECG combines convolutional morphology extraction, a bidirectional
Residual Memory Control trunk, a hybrid standard/residual memory path, and
temporal attention pooling. Under the conventional MIT-BIH DS1/DS2 protocol it
achieved the strongest performance among the evaluated classical and deep baselines in this study,
reaching accuracy $0.931$, macro F1 $0.628$, and macro AUC $0.953$. These are the
principal model results of the paper.

Controlled MIT-BIH-only subject-grouped cross-validation defines the scope of
that result. In the same flat recurrent model family, the conventional DS1/DS2
F1-F point estimate of $0.241$ contrasts with a fold-first SG-CV mean of
$0.0109$; F1-S ranges from $0.039$ to $0.696$ across subject-grouped folds.
This is protocol-sensitivity evidence, not a causal estimate of the split effect.
Reporting every fold revealed near-zero F performance and substantial S
variability that a single test score concealed.

R2 provided a modest, seed-consistent improvement in S/PAC ranking, but this did
not translate into a useful selective classifier: neither R2 nor flat met the
pre-specified S criterion on any of the three ranking-reliable folds. The
selective contribution is therefore a NO-GO. For F, low-rank errors,
patient-relative geometry, and the absence of a recoverable calibration effect
support referral as a scoped design recommendation rather than successful
automation.

The contribution is therefore twofold: ResMemECG is an effective inter-patient
ECG classifier under the conventional benchmark, and its subject-grouped
evaluation provides an explicit account of where rare-class generalisation
remains fragile. The latter does not turn this into a different model paper or
erase the former; it prevents the aggregate ResMemECG result from being
extended beyond the record groups for which evidence exists.
